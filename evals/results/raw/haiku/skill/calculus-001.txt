**Verdict: INVALID**

**Claim and assumptions:** If $f'(x) > 0$ on an interval $I$, then $f(x) > 0$ on $I$. The hypotheses are that $f$ is differentiable on $I$ and $f' > 0$ there. Nothing is assumed about the value of $f$.

**First unsupported step: Step 3.**

**Why it fails:** Step 2 is justified. If $f' > 0$ on an interval, the Mean Value Theorem gives $f(y) - f(x) = f'(\xi)(y - x) > 0$ for $x < y$ in $I$, so $f$ is strictly increasing. Step 3 then infers that $f > 0$. Strict monotonicity fixes only the *direction* of change, not the *sign* of $f$. The argument has no information about $f$'s value at any point, so the conclusion does not follow from Step 2.

**Counterexample:** Let $f(x) = x - 1$ and $I = (0, 2)$. Then $f'(x) = 1 > 0$ on $I$, but $f(0.5) = -0.5 < 0$. The hypotheses hold and the conclusion fails.

**Repair (not part of the audit verdict):** The claim becomes true with an added value condition at a left endpoint. If $I = [a, b)$ and $f(a) \ge 0$ with $f' > 0$ on $I$, then $f(x) > f(a) \ge 0$ for all $x \in (a, b)$. The original claim is false as stated.
