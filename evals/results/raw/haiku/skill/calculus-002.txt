**Claim and assumptions:** For a continuous $f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}$, there exists $x^*\in[0,1]$ with $f(x)\le f(x^*)$ for all $x\in[0,1]$. The hypotheses are that the domain is $[0,1]$ and $f$ is continuous.

**Step-by-step check:**

1. **$[0,1]$ is compact.** This is the Heine–Borel theorem, a standard result that applies directly to the closed bounded interval. Justified.
2. **$f([0,1])$ is compact.** This is the standard theorem that continuous images of compact sets are compact. It uses the hypothesis that $f$ is continuous and the conclusion of step 1. Justified.
3. **A nonempty compact subset of $\mathbb{R}$ contains its supremum, so $f$ attains a maximum.** Compact subsets of $\mathbb{R}$ are closed and bounded. By definition of the image, $s=f(x^*)$ for some $x^*\in[0,1]$, and $f(x)\le s=f(x^*)$ for all $x$. Justified.
