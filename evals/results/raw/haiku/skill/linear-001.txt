**Verdict: INVALID.** The claim is false, and the proof fails at step 2.

**Claim and assumptions.** For a real symmetric matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$, the claim is that $x^\top A x \ge 0$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$. The hypothesis is only symmetry.

**First unsupported step: step 2.** Step 1 is correct by the spectral theorem: a real symmetric matrix has real eigenvalues. Step 2 then infers that the eigenvalues are nonnegative, which does not follow. Realness of the eigenvalues says nothing about their sign, and the hypothesis (symmetry) does not give nonnegativity.

**Counterexample.** Let
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}.$$
$A$ is real and symmetric, with eigenvalues $1$ and $-1$. For $x = (0,1)^\top$,
$$x^\top A x = -1 < 0,$$
so $A$ is not PSD.

**Step 3.** The inference from step 2 to step 3 is valid in itself. If $A = Q\Lambda Q^\top$ with $Q$ orthogonal, then $x^\top A x = \sum_i \lambda_i y_i^2$ where $y = Q^\top x$, so nonnegative eigenvalues would give nonnegativity of the quadratic form. The error is therefore confined to step 2, and step 3 inherits a false premise.

**Repair (after diagnosis).** The correct statements are:
- A real symmetric matrix is PSD if and only if all its eigenvalues are nonnegative.
- The claim becomes true if "PSD" is added as a hypothesis, or if "symmetric" is replaced by "symmetric with all eigenvalues $\ge 0$."
