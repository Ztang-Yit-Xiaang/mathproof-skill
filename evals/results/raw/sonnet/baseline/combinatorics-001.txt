**Audit**

The claim is the handshake lemma, and the proof is the standard double-counting argument. It is sketched but sound.

- **Step 1:** Let I be the set of incidences (v, e, end) where an edge end attaches to v. Summing degrees over vertices counts each incidence once, so sum_v deg(v) = |I|. Under the standard convention, deg(v) is the number of edge-ends at v, which makes this step justified.
- **Step 2:** Each edge has exactly two ends. For a non-loop edge, the two ends are at distinct endpoints. For a loop, both ends are at the same vertex, which is why the loop is counted twice, matching the degree convention. So |I| = 2|E|. This is justified, and the convention is stated explicitly in the step.
- **Step 3:** Combining steps 1 and 2 gives sum_v deg(v) = 2|E|. Finiteness ensures the sums are finite. This follows directly.

**Minor remarks, not errors:**
- The proof does not spell out that "incidence" means edge-end. This is a matter of exposition and does not affect validity.
- For simple graphs the loop clause is vacuous. For multigraphs the argument still holds, since parallel edges are counted separately.

No step is unjustified, so the proof establishes the claim.
