**Claim and assumptions.** S ⊆ ℝ is nonempty and bounded (both below and above). The target is that S has a minimum, meaning some m ∈ S with m ≤ s for all s ∈ S.

**Step check**
1. Justified. A bounded set is bounded below.
2. Justified. A nonempty set that is bounded below has an infimum in ℝ, by the least-upper-bound property.
3. **Not justified, and false in general.** Completeness gives that inf S exists in ℝ. It does not give inf S ∈ S.

**Counterexample.** Take S = (0, 1). It is nonempty and bounded. Its infimum is 0, but 0 ∉ S. If m ∈ S were a minimum, then m/2 ∈ S and m/2 < m, a contradiction. So S has no minimum, and the claim itself is false, not just the proof.

**Repair (separate from the diagnosis).** Two true statements are close to the claim:
- Every nonempty set bounded below has an infimum.
- Every nonempty *closed* set bounded below, such as a nonempty compact set, contains its infimum and so has a minimum.

The missing assumption is closedness, or more generally that inf S ∈ S.
